## Gist
Dana Ingraham (core product at Harvey) disagrees that you must be "AI-pilled" to get an AI job: Harvey hires PMs for product craft (first principles, closeness to the user), and for those people AI is not hard to learn. Three guests who moved into AI-first companies in the past year (Ingraham; Hemal Shah, OpenAI → Google Cloud; Jiaona Zhang, CPO at Laurel) were not AI experts 18 months earlier. Other takeaways:
- PM skills as **historian** (why things are the way they are), **technologist** (explain complex things simply) and **anthropologist** (study an unfamiliar field until you can describe it).
- A company whose strategy keeps shifting can be healthy. It shows willingness to adapt. Companies that sound very sure of themselves are the bigger worry.
- AI gives leaders more capacity: Zhang teaches more than ever, in much less time.
- Judge a role by the skills it builds, not by whether the company will win.
- **People curation**: when a new model ships, Shah calls a small set of people (a researcher, a friend who tried it, peers at other companies).
